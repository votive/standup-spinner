# The winner is chosen before the wheel animates, not by simulating physics

A Spin selects its winner first, uniformly at random from the eligible set, and the rotation is then solved backwards so the wheel decelerates to rest with that wedge under the pointer.

The alternative — apply a random impulse, simulate friction, read off wherever it stops — is not more fair, only less controllable. It can come to rest straddling a wedge boundary, it makes spin duration unpredictable, and quantising the result back onto a wedge reintroduces the same correction by a more confusing route. Deciding first means a consistent, well-tuned deceleration curve every time and an unambiguous landing.

The randomness is uniform over eligible participants and is not weighted, seeded, or adjusted. The pre-selection is a rendering convenience, not a thumb on the scale.
